How it works

Four steps, each one checkable

TapeJudge turns a scoring rule into a circuit, records the evidence, and leaves the arithmetic open to anyone.

01

Freeze the rule

An organiser writes the criteria and weights. TapeJudge compiles them into a circuit and hashes it. After freezing, the rule cannot change.

02

Record evaluations

Each judge scores every entry, criterion by criterion. A submitted score is committed and cannot be edited.

03

Compute the ranking

The ranking is recomputed from the committed scores by the same evaluator every time it is read. No stored total, no hidden weight.

04

Verify independently

Anyone opens the public verify page, sees the frozen rule and the inputs, and reproduces the ranking for free.

{# ── Why this matters (dark band) ──────────────────────────────────────── #}

Why it matters

Right now, results are a claim. TapeJudge makes them a record.

  • Rules live in spreadsheets. A weighting can change after entries close, and no entrant can prove it did not.
  • Scores live in private documents. Participants see a final number, not the arithmetic or the inputs behind it.
  • Disputes become arguments. Without a fixed rule and recorded inputs, "why did I lose?" has no factual answer.
  • TapeJudge changes the object of trust. The rule is frozen, the inputs are recorded, the ranking is reproducible. You no longer have to take the organiser's word for the arithmetic.
{# ── What it does / does not prove ─────────────────────────────────────── #}

Honest boundaries

What TapeJudge proves — and what it does not

It proves

  • The rule could not change after it was frozen.
  • Every score maps to a committed input, bound to a judge identity.
  • The ranking follows from those inputs and the frozen rule.
  • Anyone can reproduce the result from public data.

It does not prove

  • That a judge was impartial or independent.
  • That an entry is original, or a score deserved.
  • That the criteria were the right ones to choose.

Bias and collusion are human problems. TapeJudge makes the acting identity and the arithmetic auditable — so a dispute is about facts, not about who to believe.

Where X Layer fits

The frozen rule is a TapeOut circuit. An organiser can publish it to X Layer as a funded, signed transaction; TapeJudge prepares that call and confirms it by reading the circuit back — byte-for-byte against the frozen netlist. The app never sends the transaction itself, and never labels a local circuit as on-chain.
